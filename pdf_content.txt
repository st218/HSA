PSA for Hermès Bags: Decision-Grade Trust
Infrastructure Memo + Build Plan for HSA
Executive summary
0) Executive decision: Proceed with constraints — run a tightly-scoped 12–18 month build with explicit
kill criteria and a trust-first operating model (authentication + rating/grading + guarantee + chain-of-
custody artifact), not a GMV-first marketplace.
What matters (and what must be true)
HSA  can  be  “the  PSA  of  Hermès”  only  if  it  becomes  the  reference  standard for  (i)  authenticity
determination, and (ii)  auditable, repeatable condition/originality grading that becomes legible to
buyers, resellers, insurers, and marketplaces. PSA’s defensibility comes from (a) a widely accepted numeric
grade signal, (b) a tamper-evident physical artifact bound to a database-verified certificate number, and (c) a
financial guarantee + error-correction machinery that market participants can underwrite into pricing
behavior. PSA explicitly commits to grading “under PSA grading standards,” uses “two or more… graders”
who “must agree upon a grade,” and offers a buyback/refund mechanism if a grade is later determined
incorrect. 
HSA must replicate those trust mechanics for Hermès bags, but with redesigns for (1) high-value, repairable
goods  where  “state  changes”  (spa/repair,  component  swaps)  are  common,  and  (2)  an  adversarial
environment where simple certificates and naïve NFC tags fail quickly.
Biggest risks (adversarial framing)
The  primary  failure  mode  is  not  demand.  It  is  standard  failure:  inconsistent  grading,  tag  decoupling
(certificate → bag mismatch), or guarantee blow-ups that collapse trust. Secondary risks are brand/IP
pressure (luxury tends to litigate more aggressively than hobby collectibles), and counterfeit adaptation
that targets HSA’s weakest link (usually chain-of-custody and re-sale verification surfaces).
Key numbers (Verified vs Estimated vs Unknown)
Verified (primary sources): -  PSA/Collectors Universe FY2020 performance: net revenues  $78.891M,
gross profit $45.236M (gross margin ~57.3%), operating income $14.102M (operating margin ~17.9%). 
-  Collectors  Universe  segment  profitability  (FY2020): Coins  generated  $41.599M net  revenues  with

Falsification checklist (30–60 day tests; pass/fail thresholds)
These are designed as kill switches, not vanity KPIs:
Inter-rater reliability: on n=120 bags (balanced across condition tiers), achieve ≥85% agreement
within ±1 condition notch and ≥70% exact-match on final condition tier after protocol training; 
fail if <75% within ±1. 
Counterfeit catch-rate (seeded red team): insert n=40 known counterfeits/superfakes (sourced
legally for testing) across categories; pass if 0 are certified authentic and ≤5% become
“inconclusive”; fail if ≥1 passes as authentic. 
Tag security (lab attack): attempt clone/replay/tag transplant on n=30 tags; pass if successful
counterfeit verification is 0% under documented attacker models; fail if any attack yields a “valid”
verification state. 
Certificate-to-bag binding audit: for n=200 certified bags, randomly re-run verification at 2 weeks;
pass if ≥99.5% resolve to correct bag identity + state; fail if any systematic mismatch appears. 
Dispute handling SLA: resolve ≥90% of disputes within 10 business days; fail if median >15
business days (trust corrosion). 
Dealer adoption (standard credibility): sign ≥6 high-velocity Hermès resellers to written “use-
grade-in-listing” commitments (or API integration LOIs); fail if <3.

Definitions & taxonomy
Definitions & taxonomy (required)
Authentication = a determination that an item is genuine (or not) based on evidence and expert judgment;
it is not “title.” eBay explicitly distinguishes that its authenticity certificate is a “tool” and “does not represent
title to the physical good.” 
Grading = a standardized evaluation of physical state/quality (often numeric) intended to be comparable
across items; PSA grades on a 1–10 scale and positions its grade as a market signal. 
Rating = a categorical or tiered condition signal (often not as granular as a formal grade). 
Certificate = a verifiable record asserting authentication and/or grade; for resilience it must be bound to an
immutable identifier and ideally to evidence (photos, measurements). Entrupy certificates are hosted on
Entrupy’s servers and meant to be validated by entering the certificate link. 
Chain-of-custody = documented control states describing when the certifier had physical possession vs
when the item is “out of custody,” which changes what can be guaranteed.
Tamper-evident = physical design where attempts to open/alter leave evidence; PSA describes holder
tamper evidence via “frosting” and changes in plastic rigidity after sonic weld violation. 
Registry = a database of certified items and their attributes; PSA provides certification verification and
encourages public cert checks. 
Population report = aggregate data describing how many items exist in each grade; PSA markets its
Population Report as a core collector resource. 
SLA = a service level agreement defining turnaround + dispute handling, essential because PSA and others
explicitly warn turnaround times are estimates and can change with capacity. 
Loss ratio = for HSA, payouts + refunds + dispute costs divided by revenue or declared value; must be
bounded for a viable guarantee product.

Corporate structure & leadership / ownership shifts
Collectors Universe (public) historically owned PSA and PCGS; it disclosed segment reporting across Coins,
Trading Cards & Autographs, and “Other,” showing authentication is the core revenue engine. 
The investor group led by Nat Turner completed acquisition in Feb 2021 (take-private). 
Collectors’ current leadership page lists Nat Turner as Chairman & CEO and Steve Cohen among board
members, and lists brands including PSA, PSA Vault, PCGS, Beckett, SGC, and Card Ladder. 
Inflection points that plausibly form today’s moat (mechanism-linked)
First, PSA’s Set Registry (2001) converts grading from “quality label” into a status game with durable network
effects: collectors compete on registry sets, increasing willingness to pay for grading and decreasing
willingness to switch standards. 
Second, guarantee + error-correction policy is not marketing fluff; PSA explicitly promises it will buy a card
or refund grade-value differences if PSA later determines grade/authenticity issues under its standards.
That converts the grade into an insurable economic claim. 
Third, physical tamper evidence + database verification reduces fraud surface: PSA emphasizes sonic-weld
tamper evidence and public cert verification pathways. 
Fourth, pricing/turnaround tiers + backlog dynamics create a capacity moat: SEC filings explain that faster
service commands higher fees and that a portion of cost of revenues is relatively fixed short-term, enabling
operating leverage at high volumes—if trust survives. 
Fifth, distribution integrations (eBay, dealer programs) spread the standard by embedding authentication
into transaction defaults; PSA describes eBay checkout integration where PSA authenticates the card and
verifies condition matches listing before grading.

Micro/internal mechanisms
Trust formation mechanics (why the market accepted PSA)
PSA’s trust proposition is a bundle: (i) a standard, (ii) process controls, and (iii) financial responsibility. The
FAQ makes two operationally critical claims: multi-rater review (“two or more… graders… must agree”) and
economic accountability (buyback/refund policy). 
That  combination  changes  market  behavior:  it  makes  “PSA  grade”  a  tradable  signal  and  gives
counterparties a common language for sight-unseen trade.
Network effects: registry + population report + adoption loops
PSA’s  Population  Report  provides  public  supply  distribution  by  grade,  which  becomes  price  discovery
infrastructure (scarcity + liquidity). 
The Set Registry creates a structured competition that rewards grading participation and drives repeat
submissions. 
The loop is: more submissions → richer population data + more registry competition → greater perceived
legitimacy of PSA grades → more marketplace quoting in PSA terms → more submissions.
Pricing + capacity control: tiers/turnaround as demand throttle
Collectors Universe filings are explicit: turnaround times requested affect revenue because the company
“charge[s] higher fees for faster service times,” and higher ASP in a quarter came from customers paying for
improved turnaround. 
PSA’s public service-tier menu makes the same strategy legible to customers and enables continuous
repricing to match volume to capacity. 
Workflow/QA signals (what can be verified publicly)
While PSA does not fully disclose internal SOPs, it does make verifiable claims about:
- Certification verification via app/QR and cert lookup. 
- Reholder workflow that includes authenticity checks on the holder and card before re-encapsulation,
implying chain-of-custody thinking and anti-counterfeit measures even for the holder itself. 
Failure modes and mitigations
Backlog can destroy trust if it incentivizes corner-cutting. Collectors Universe disclosed a “record backlog” of
card submissions as of Sept 30, 2020. 
PSA’s control lever is pricing and service mix (charge more for speed), but the unsolved piece is that grading
remains labor-intensive and thus exposed to training drift, fatigue, and incentive distortion. PSA’s multi-
grader agreement rule is one mitigation, but HSA must design deeper auditability given higher claim
severity for bags. 
Macro/external mechanisms
Market structure shift and why third-party standards become necessary
Collectors Universe describes authentication/grading as facilitating sales because it provides “a high level of
assurance as to the authenticity and quality” of items being bought/sold, linking demand for grading to
transactional behavior. 
This is the macro logic HSA must exploit: as Hermès resale transactions become increasingly remote and
cross-border, third-party standards become a prerequisite to liquidity.

B1) Similarities/differences that matter for HSA (evidence-based where possible)
Where PSA transfers cleanly (similarities)
Third-party  certification  becomes  economically  valuable  when  it  enables  remote  trade  by  providing
“assurance as to authenticity and quality” that facilitates buying/selling behavior. That is explicitly how
Collectors Universe describes the driver of grading demand. 
Hermès resale has the same structure: high price dispersion, asymmetric information, and high fraud
payoff.
Where Hermès breaks the PSA model (differences that force redesign)
Repairability and “state changes” are endemic
Unlike  cards  in  slabs,  bags  are  living  objects:  spa  treatments,  re-dyeing,  hardware  swaps,  strap
replacement, and refurbishment can change both condition and authenticity evidence. Chinese reporting
highlights a growing “旧包改造” (old bag modification) business, with shops accepting customer-provided
bags and charging ~1000–3000 RMB depending on work; it also describes risks of “一眼假” outcomes and
trademark issues. 
Implication: HSA must grade originality state (original vs repaired vs modified) and define re-auth triggers.
Luxury demand is value-preservation oriented (a driver of grading adoption)
Tencent Marketing Insight × BCG’s 2024 report states China mainland consumers’ luxury spending is
estimated at 5,720亿元 in 2024 (+4%), and notes categories with stronger value-preservation attributes (珠
宝、皮具) leading growth. 
Implication: Hermès buyers already reason in “value retention” terms—fertile ground for a credible grade
signal if it is trusted.
Secondhand/grey-market complexity raises authentication stakes
A Reuters report describes China’s growing second-hand and grey luxury markets, including discounts on
platforms like DeWu (Dewu/得物) and estimates the grey market at ~$57B annually, emphasizing price gaps

Non-transferable (must redesign) - Bags are repairable and modifiable. HSA must define state models
(validity depends on custody and subsequent interventions). PSA can rely on slab integrity; HSA cannot.
- Physical “slab” isn’t viable. A bag slab is socially unacceptable and impractical; instead, the “artifact” must
be a minimal, non-damaging seal + digital record.
- Claim severity is larger. A counterfeit graded “authentic” can be a $10k–$200k event; HSA must be more
conservative than PSA in initial guarantee scope and must embrace “inconclusive.”
Mandatory: PSA slab equivalent for bags (“Digital Slab” options and evaluation)
Option set 1) Certificate-only (paper + QR) — Rejected. Certificate substitution and post-auth tampering
are  trivial.  Certilogo  explicitly  warns  that  finding  a  code  online  or  via  photo  “does  not  guarantee”
authenticity; you must have the product to check it. 
2) Naïve NFC hangtag (static UID / static URL) — Rejected. Academic work notes NFC Forum lacks a tag
authentication mechanism; in that absence, NFC tags cannot provide secure product identification, and
cloning becomes central. 
3) Cryptographic NFC + tamper-evident physical attachment (“Digital Slab” recommended) — Proposed
HSA baseline. NXP describes AES-based SUN messages where each tap yields a unique message, verified by
the backend host. 
4) In-custody vault-only validity — Complement, not replacement. Works for marketplaces who can keep
custody (akin to vault models), but HSA must also support out-of-custody verification if it wants to be a
category standard (not just a warehouse standard).
NFC / tagging requirements (deep, threat-modeled)
Threat model (minimum set) - Tag cloning (copy identifier)
- UID copying / emulation
- Replay attacks (capture and replay a “valid” scan)
- Certificate substitution (swap cert for a different authentic bag)
- Tag removal/reattachment
- Adhesive replacement (fake seal)
- “Donor bag” tag transplant (move tag to counterfeit bag)
- Backend API abuse (scrape cert IDs; brute-force verification endpoints)
Why naïve NFC fails (plain English, source-backed) - NFC Forum tags can carry NDEF data but NFC Forum
does not provide a native authentication mechanism; without cryptographic authentication, secure product
identification is not achievable. 
- Brands and authentication systems warn that codes seen online/photos are insufficient: a code can be
copied, and verification must be tied to physical possession and secure checks.

response or tap-unique cryptographic message generation. NXP’s NTAG 424 DNA describes AES-128 SUN

B3) Comparables & adjacent attempts (why hasn’t this become standardized already?)

StockX (sneakers/streetwear/handbags) - Trust mechanism (promise language): “Verified by StockX” and
